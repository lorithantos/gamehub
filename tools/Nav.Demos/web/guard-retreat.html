What the doctrine decided

    Four things to watch

    Nothing happens for the first half, and that is the setup. Three of the six park inside the enemy’s reach and three do not, so at ticks 57 and 147 the near side becomes regulars and then veterans. Rank is not handed out here — there is no way to hand it out. It is earned by standing somewhere, and you can watch it being earned.

    Then the pair, and it looks wrong. At 180 a veteran is scratched to 0.65 and leaves at once. At 184 a rookie is hurt worse, to 0.50, and stands there. The healthier unit walks off the line while the hurt one holds it, because rank raises the retreat threshold: the good unit is the one you cannot replace.

    Then the reserve inverts it. At 240 three more are hurt at once and only two may go, because four of six must stay standing. Now the veteran is the one that stays — at 0.30, far under its own 0.70 — while both rookies take the places, to different pads. A veteran’s place is the line: it earns faster where the enemy is, and its standing there is what makes the position survivable for the rookies beside it. Unit 5 waits 36 ticks at a quarter health for a place to come free.

    The line never empties, and everyone comes back. A unit at a pad is still a member of the squad; once repaired the doctrine recalls it and it takes a place in the formation again. Across the whole run the position is never held by fewer than four.
