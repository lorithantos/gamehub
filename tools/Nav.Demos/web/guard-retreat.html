What the doctrine decided

    Four things to watch

    The enemy advances once, at tick 100. One cell. From where it started, its reach covered the front arc of the ring and not the back, so three units were earning rank and bleeding while three were doing neither. After the move it covers all six. Nobody is hurt by the move itself — the line just stops having a sheltered half, and everything after is the consequence.

    Being good at the job is what gets you sent to the rear. Units 0, 2 and 3 are in reach from the start, so they are both the ones who bleed and the ones who are promoted. At 57 and 59 their retreat threshold rises from 0.40 to 0.55 along with the rank, and they fall back at 0.54. As rookies they would have stood there for another fourteen hundredths of health first. The promotion is what pulled them out.

    The reserve has a price, and it is on screen as a number. A unit should leave the moment it falls under its own threshold. It actually leaves when a place comes free, and the gap is health spent standing in the line waiting for one. Unit 3 goes at 0.44, eleven hundredths past its threshold. Then the real one: unit 5 goes at 0.42 against a threshold of 0.70 — 0.28 past it — because by then it was the highest-ranked unit in the queue, and the reserve spends its places on the lowest rank first. Two rookies at 0.51 and 0.50 went ahead of it. A veteran’s place is the line; here that rule costs one veteran 0.28 of health, and nothing staged it.

    Then it settles into a rotation, which is the point. Veterans cycle at their 0.70 threshold on trips of about twenty ticks — out, mend, back — while the line goes on holding. All six finish as veterans, all six earned it under fire, and the position is never held by fewer than four.
