What the doctrine decided

    Four things to watch

    The reserve has a price, and in the first wave it is paid in a unit. A unit should leave the moment it falls under its own threshold. It actually leaves when a place comes free, because the reserve keeps five standing whoever is hurt. At tick 23 guard 1 — a buggy, the thinnest armour on the line — falls back at 0.20 against a threshold of 0.40, twenty hundredths past it, and waits. It is destroyed two ticks later by the rocket bike it was still standing in front of. Usually the overrun costs health. Here it cost the unit, and nothing staged it.

    Neither pad is visible from the line, and that is the point of the fog. They sit in opposite corners, twenty-two cells out — three times what a tank can see. No guard ever lays eyes on one. They are known because a pad watches the ground it stands on, and that is the only reason the retreat works at all: with pads granting no vision, the same run rotates nobody through repair and reports a worst overrun of zero, while looking otherwise identical. A whole mechanic disappears without an error.

    A repair trip is now a real absence. Guard 2 crosses its threshold at tick 172, reaches the pad at 199, and rejoins the line at 218 at 0.82 health — forty-six ticks away, about eleven seconds, out of a line of eight. On the old map the same errand took roughly twenty. That distance is what the bigger board was for: the rotation only means something when leaving costs time.

    Rank changes who gets pulled, and it is earned in front of you. Guard 2 becomes a veteran at tick 178 on the damage it dealt to the second wave. From then on it is pulled at 0.70 rather than 0.40 — it goes again at 372 on a scratch of 0.64 — because a veteran is worth mending early and a rookie is not. Two units finish as veterans, both under fire, and the position is never held by fewer than six.

    Recorded by Nav.Demos from a live MovementSystem run and replayed frame for frame — positions, health and every decision below are the simulation’s own, not an animation of one. 49×33 cells, 520 ticks — about 98 by 66 metres over two minutes ten — collision-free throughout. The replay draws the true board, not either side’s view of it: the units are under fog, the camera is not. And the waves are too light — eighteen attackers destroyed for one guard lost. That is a known debt, left alone deliberately, because the retreat and casualty-response work will change the answer.
